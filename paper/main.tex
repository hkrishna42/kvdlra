\begin{abstract}
We test whether the \emph{basis-update \& Galerkin} (BUG) integrator for
dynamical low-rank approximation (DLRA) --- a tool from numerical analysis for
evolving low-rank factorizations of matrix ODEs --- is a good \emph{streaming}
compressor for the key/value (KV) cache of autoregressive transformers. We show
that a rank-adaptive BUG subspace tracker applied to the (pre-RoPE) KV stream is
a \emph{near-oracle} low-rank approximator: on real Llama-3.2-1B KV it matches
the truncated-SVD (Eckart--Young) optimum to within $1.01$--$1.03\times$ and beats
incremental SVD and Oja's rule at matched rank. Wired into NVIDIA's
\texttt{kvpress} as \texttt{BUGPress}, it preserves greedy generation and
degrades perplexity gracefully with rank (near-baseline at $4\times$ per-token
compression). Because low-rank compresses the \emph{feature} axis and
quantization the \emph{bit} axis, the two compose: we combine BUG with TurboQuant
residual quantization and sweep the memory--perplexity frontier against SnapKV
and Expected~Attention. We report honestly where BUG wins and where the
heavy-tailed KV spectrum limits pure low-rank compression.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The KV cache dominates memory in long-context LLM inference. Most compressors
either \emph{evict} tokens (SnapKV, H2O, Expected~Attention) or \emph{quantize}
per-element (KIVI, KVQuant, TurboQuant). We instead ask whether the cache's
low-rank structure can be tracked \emph{online} with a principled integrator
rather than a heuristic. DLRA's BUG integrator \cite{ceruti2022unconventional,
ceruti2022rankadaptive} evolves an orthonormal basis and a small core under a
matrix flow with robust error guarantees; we specialize it to the data-fitting
flow of a column-streamed KV matrix.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(i) A streaming rank-adaptive BUG subspace tracker for KV, shown near-oracle on
real KV streams; (ii) \texttt{BUGPress}, a \texttt{kvpress}-compatible press with
a pre-RoPE operating point and a GPU-capable blocked-BUG kernel; (iii) a
composition of BUG with TurboQuant quantization and a memory--perplexity study
against eviction baselines; (iv) an honest account of the regime where pure
low-rank compression is limited by the KV spectrum.

\section{Method}
\paragraph{Convention.} A per-layer key tensor of shape
$(H, T, D)$ is factored as $M \in \mathbb{R}^{HD \times T}$ (rows $=$ features
$HD{=}512$, columns $=$ tokens $T$); a new token is a new column.

\paragraph{Streaming BUG tracker.} We maintain an orthonormal basis
$U \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times r}$ and a square-root core $B$ with $BB^\top =
U^\top M M^\top U$. Each new column (or block of columns) triggers an augmented
BUG step: range-augment $U$ with the residual direction, Galerkin-project the
grown data onto the augmented basis, and truncate by a Frobenius-tail criterion
(rank cap $r$ or tolerance $\theta$). The reconstruction is the orthogonal
projection $\hat M = U U^\top M$. A \emph{blocked} variant processes $b$ columns
per step ($b{=}1$ recovers the per-token tracker; $b{=}T$ recovers the SVD
oracle), enabling batched QR/SVD on GPU. All core linear algebra is fp32 even
when storage is bf16.

\paragraph{Pre-RoPE operating point.} HuggingFace caches \emph{post}-RoPE keys,
which are markedly less low-rank. \texttt{BUGPress} factors the recomputed
pre-RoPE keys and re-applies RoPE to the reconstruction, roughly halving the
reconstruction error at matched rank.

\paragraph{Composition with TurboQuant.} The low-rank model stores $U$
($n\times r$) and per-token coordinates $C = U^\top M$ ($r \times T$). We
quantize the coordinate columns with TurboQuant \cite{zandieh2025turboquant}: a
fixed random rotation makes coordinates near-independent and Beta-distributed, an
MSE-optimal per-coordinate Lloyd--Max quantizer then achieves distortion within
$\approx 2.7\times$ the information-theoretic floor, and a 1-bit QJL residual
gives unbiased inner-product estimates. Because BUG factors are \emph{pre}-RoPE,
the fixed quantization rotation and RoPE never need to commute (RoPE is applied
last, to the reconstruction). Our reconstruct-then-attend press uses the
PolarQuant scalar stage; the 1-bit QJL residual de-biases an
estimator-at-attention path we implement and test but leave to future work.

\section{Experiments}
\paragraph{Setup.} Llama-3.2-1B (ungated \texttt{unsloth} mirror, config-identical),
fp32, WikiText-2. Perplexity uses a \emph{prefill-then-score} protocol: the
context cache is compressed, then continuation tokens are scored against the
compressed cache (necessary because \texttt{kvpress} compresses in a
post-attention hook).

\paragraph{Near-oracle reconstruction (Week 2).} Streaming BUG is within
$1.01$--$1.03\times$ of the truncated-SVD oracle at every rank and beats
incremental SVD everywhere; pre-RoPE roughly halves the error at matched budget;
BUG beats Oja's rule by $1.3$--$3.0\times$.

\paragraph{Generation \& perplexity (Week 3).} \texttt{BUGPress} preserves greedy
generation (byte-identical at moderate rank; coherent, on-topic drift at
aggressive rank; no divergence). WikiText-2 perplexity (ctx 1024, 16 windows,
baseline $12.65$): rank~128 $+0.83$ ($4\times$), rank~64 $+1.17$ ($8\times$),
rank~32 $+3.57$, rank~16 $+5.47$. Pre-RoPE beats post-RoPE across the useful
compression range (rank~$\le 64$).

\paragraph{Memory--perplexity frontier (Week 4).}
Quantizing the BUG coordinates at 4 bits roughly halves the stored cache for a
negligible perplexity cost (rank~64: $0.191\times/{+}1.17 \to 0.099\times/{+}1.20$;
rank~128: $0.378\times/{+}0.83 \to 0.193\times/{+}0.94$); 2 bits is the cliff.
Crucially, at a fixed memory budget the composition beats pure low-rank:
rank-64/4-bit ($0.099\times$, ppl $13.86$) versus rank-32/fp16 ($0.097\times$, ppl
$16.22$) --- quantization and low-rank multiply rather than substitute.
Under a \emph{fair} comparison --- every mechanism quantized equally, since
quantization is orthogonal to eviction (Fig.~\ref{fig:hero}) --- BUG+TurboQuant is
\textbf{competitive but not dominant} on WikiText-2 perplexity at ctx~1024:
Expected~Attention$\times$TurboQuant and SnapKV$\times$TurboQuant sit on or ahead
of BUG's Pareto frontier through the mid-aggressive band ($0.08$--$0.18\times$),
and pure 4-bit TurboQuant is near-lossless at $0.25\times$. BUG is best only at the
\emph{extreme} edge ($<0.07\times$), where token eviction becomes catastrophic and
low-rank degrades gracefully. (An earlier draft's "beats SOTA at $10\times$"
compared quantized BUG against fp16 eviction and is retracted.) BUG's case rests on
the extreme-compression edge, retrieval parity (\S\ref{fig:hero}), the streaming/
online deployment niche, and the expectation that its fixed-basis overhead
amortizes at longer context --- not on winning short-context perplexity.

\section{Limitations (honest)}
Absolute low-rank compression is moderate: even pre-RoPE, the heavy-tailed KV
spectrum limits pure low-rank to a few dozen--hundred useful rank; aggressive
ranks cost real perplexity. Reported memory for the in-place press is
\emph{nominal} unless factors are stored explicitly; the fixed basis $U$
amortizes only for long context. Perplexity on WikiText-2 is not a long-context
benchmark (LongBench/RULER) and results are at 1B scale.
