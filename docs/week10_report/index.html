01What this is, in plain words

When an LLM reads a long prompt it stores a running summary of every token — the KV-cache. It gets huge and dominates memory, so people compress it. Two families:

Eviction (MorphKV, SnapKV, ExpectedAttention) throws away "unimportant" tokens and keeps the rest exactly. Low-rank (our BUG, plus Palu, ThinK, ShadowKV) keeps a compressed summary of everything instead of dropping tokens. We put all 7 on one honest scale: quality vs. how much memory they use, at 32K and 64K tokens, on Llama-3.2-1B and Llama-3.1-8B.

Every method's memory is counted the same way (float-equivalents per layer). Crucially, ShadowKV's trick of parking values in CPU RAM is not treated as free — a stored float costs the same wherever it lives. That's what keeps the comparison fair.

03The twist: can it still find things?

Perplexity rewards keeping the general gist. But long-context tasks are often about retrieval — "find the one passcode buried 20,000 tokens back." We hid a needle mid-context, compressed, then asked for it. This is where the story flips.

04Who wins where

No method wins everywhere. Three honest regimes:

RegimeWinnerWhy
Extreme compression
below ~10% of the cache		 BUG (only option) A rank-32 summary reaches 3% of the cache. Eviction physically can't go there — its floor is ~11% (it must keep whole tokens). BUG pays a quality premium but it's the only thing in this zone.
Moderate compression, long context
~11–60% of the cache, 32K–64K		 MorphKV / SnapKV (eviction) At long context, keeping 10–25% of the right tokens is nearly lossless (7.54 vs 7.62 full). Eviction is remarkably strong here — matching the project's honest Week-4 finding.
Retrieval @ 32K
find a specific fact		 BUG + exact tier (Week-11) BUG's low-rank summary alone is too blurry — 0% at every rank (the rank-vs-context wall). But Week-11 flips this: keeping the most surprising tokens exact reaches 100% at 0.009× memory, ~11× cheaper than ExpectedAttention (100% at 0.10×). Across all 4 tasks the summary-plus-exact-tier variant (bugslash) is the robust winner; exact-tier-only handles only the single needle.
Important nuance vs. earlier weeks. At moderate context (≤4K, tested separately) BUG's low-rank summary does win retrieval — it recovers needles eviction forgets. Its low-rank summary still can't rebuild a sharp fact at 32K (the rank-vs-context wall). Week-11 gets retrieval at 32K a different way — a surprise-selected exact tier (100% at 0.009×) — so the durable lesson holds: BUG's edge is extreme compression and its outlier signal, not long-range low-rank reconstruction.

06Honest caveats

  • Retrieval & QA at 16K, and ShadowKV, landed in Week 11. The perplexity frontier (1B+8B, 32K+64K) was already complete; Week-11 then fixed harness bugs that had silently dropped MorphKV/SnapKV and repaired ShadowKV, so the full 4-task RULER at 16K/8B and LongBench qasper now run for all methods. Details in docs/week11.md. (Multi-key/value at 32K and the 1B row remain honest follow-ups.)
  • ShadowKV can't beat 0.5× here — it keeps every value, just in CPU RAM, and we count that honestly. Its win is GPU-headroom/throughput, an axis we deliberately don't reward.
  • Palu is the per-head variant (each attention head gets its own tiny subspace). It's weak because it can't share structure across heads the way BUG's pooled subspace does. A grouped Palu would sit closer to BUG.
  • Small trial counts on retrieval; perplexity is on a held-out continuation window. Absolute numbers are indicative — the ordering is the claim.
